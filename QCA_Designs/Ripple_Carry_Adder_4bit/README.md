# QCA 4-bit Ripple Carry Adder (RCA) Implementation

## 1. What a Ripple Carry Adder Is

A **Ripple Carry Adder (RCA)** is an essential digital arithmetic circuit that performs multi-bit binary addition of two unsigned binary numbers. In binary digital systems, addition of multi-bit numbers requires that each bit position sum its corresponding addend bits along with any carry generated by the preceding less significant bit position. 

In a Ripple Carry Adder, the carry output from each individual bit stage "ripples" directly into the carry input of the subsequent more significant bit stage. This simple, elegant modular architecture allows $N$-bit additions to be constructed systematically by cascading $N$ identical 1-bit Full Adder building blocks.

---

## 2. Why Four Full Adders Are Used

To compute the arithmetic sum of two 4-bit numbers:
$$A = (A_3, A_2, A_1, A_0)_2, \quad B = (B_3, B_2, B_1, B_0)_2$$
with a possible initial carry-in $C_{in}$, four bit positions (Bit 0, Bit 1, Bit 2, Bit 3) must be evaluated:
1. **Stage 0 ($\text{FA}_0$):** Adds $A_0 + B_0 + C_{in} \implies S_0, C_1$.
2. **Stage 1 ($\text{FA}_1$):** Adds $A_1 + B_1 + C_1 \implies S_1, C_2$.
3. **Stage 2 ($\text{FA}_2$):** Adds $A_2 + B_2 + C_2 \implies S_2, C_3$.
4. **Stage 3 ($\text{FA}_3$):** Adds $A_3 + B_3 + C_3 \implies S_3, C_{out}$.

Each bit slice requires three binary inputs ($A_k, B_k, C_k$) and two outputs ($S_k, C_{k+1}$). A Half Adder cannot be used for higher stages because it accepts only two inputs; thus, four complete 1-bit Full Adders are required.

---

## 3. Carry Propagation Mechanism

Carry propagation forms the critical path in any Ripple Carry Adder:
- The initial carry $C_{in}$ enters $\text{FA}_0$ at $t = 0$.
- $\text{FA}_0$ calculates intermediate carry $C_1 = M(A_0, B_0, C_{in})$.
- $C_1$ travels across the inter-stage transmission wire into $\text{FA}_1$.
- $\text{FA}_1$ evaluates $C_2 = M(A_1, B_1, C_1)$ and transmits it to $\text{FA}_2$.
- $\text{FA}_2$ evaluates $C_3 = M(A_2, B_2, C_2)$ and transmits it to $\text{FA}_3$.
- Finally, $\text{FA}_3$ evaluates the primary overflow carry $C_{out} = M(A_3, B_3, C_3)$.

Because each stage cannot finalize its carry-out until its carry-in has stabilized, the carry chain ripples through all four stages in sequence.

## 6. Boolean Behavior & Mathematical Formulation

The mathematical equation implemented by the circuit is:

$$A + B + C_{in} = 16 \cdot C_{out} + S = 16 \cdot C_{out} + \sum_{k=0}^3 S_k \cdot 2^k$$

For each stage $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, the Boolean logic equations are:
$$C_{k+1} = M(A_k, B_k, C_k) = A_k B_k + B_k C_k + A_k C_k$$
$$S_k = A_k \oplus B_k \oplus C_k = M(\overline{C_{k+1}}, M(A_k, B_k, \overline{C_k}), C_k)$$

## 7. QCA Physical Implementation Details

The circuit is realized in `RCA_4bit.qca` by cascading four instances of the Tougaw-Lent Full Adder architecture:
- **Total Cell Count:** 315 cells
- **Cell Dimensions:** 18 nm × 18 nm, 5 nm dot diameter, 20 nm center-to-center pitch.
- **Bounding Box Dimensions:** $1338.00\text{ nm} \times 218.00\text{ nm}$ ($x \in [51.0, 1389.0]\text{ nm}$, $y \in [91.0, 309.0]\text{ nm}$).
- **Calculated Area:** $0.291684\ \mu\text{m}^2$ ($291,684\text{ nm}^2$).
- **Inter-Stage Connections:** Horizontal carry wires route from $(x_k + 340, 120)$ downward through $(x_k + 380, 220)$ to enter the subsequent stage's $C_{in}$ input at $(x_k + 400, 220)$ with zero cross-talk.

---

## 8. Clocking & Pipelining Strategy

Information flow is enforced by a 4-phase adiabatic clocking scheme:
- Each Full Adder stage transitions across 4 clock phases: Zone 0 (Inputs) $\to$ Zone 1 (Routing) $\to$ Zone 2 (M1, M2 Evaluation) $\to$ Zone 3 (Sum M3 Evaluation & Carry Transmission).
- Stage $k$ operates on clock cycle $k$.
- Total circuit latency is **4.00 clock cycles** (16 clock phases) from $C_{in}$ to $C_{out}$.
- Because cells in the Hold phase act as memory latches, the circuit supports pipelined operation where new operands can be injected once per clock cycle.

## 10. Current Limitations & Scope

1. **Ripple Carry Delay Scaling:** As word size $N$ increases (e.g., 64-bit or 128-bit words in HE), linear ripple carry delay ($\mathcal{O}(N)$ cycles) becomes the throughput bottleneck. Future iterations will investigate Carry Lookahead (CLA) or Carry Select (CSLA) architectures in QCA.
